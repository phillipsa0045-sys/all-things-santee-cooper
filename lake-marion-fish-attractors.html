Lake Marion's Hidden Fishing Structures: 16 Documented Fish Attractors

Exact SCDNR dataset coordinates and a practical guide to understanding what is beneath your boat.

There's more under Lake Marion than old channels

Some of the structure that anglers fish around was deliberately placed there to create habitat. Using SCDNR freshwater fish-attractor data, All Things Santee Cooper has identified 16 documented locations numbered 21 through 36 around the eastern portion of Lake Marion. Here are the coordinates, the materials listed in the dataset and the fishing questions worth asking at each location.

These aren't random brush piles

SCDNR has worked with Santee Cooper and other partners to improve fish habitat across Lake Marion and Lake Moultrie. Santee Cooper documented its role delivering concrete pipe to the lake bottom by barge and crane. The SCDNR program also uses brush, trees, bamboo and other habitat materials. Not every fish attractor is manufactured; the material type can vary and sites can be supplemented over time.

Why fish relate to structure

These habitat sites may give small fish cover, provide surfaces for aquatic organisms and attract baitfish. Crappie and panfish may use vertical cover, bass can use edges as ambush points, and catfish may move through to feed. But an attractor is not a guaranteed fishing hotspot. Depth, season, water conditions, forage and the state of the structure determine how useful it is.

How I would fish these sites

Rather than run straight to the waypoint and anchor, I'd approach carefully with sonar. Examine nearby drop-offs, channel shoulders and bottom contour; then look for bait and fish. Identify whether fish are suspended, close to the structure or moving around its edges. A crappie presentation may be very different from the approach for a blue catfish. The GPS puts you in the right area; observations tell you what to do once you arrive.

What comes next

We'll examine these locations individually, where possible combining published data with sonar and on-water observations. The goal is to determine how each structure relates to depth, nearby contours, seasonal forage and practical fishing—not to declare every numbered site productive without evidence.

Be careful around the habitat

Do not anchor into a fish attractor. Anchors and lines can snag, damage habitat or be lost. These coordinates are for locating potential fishing habitat, not navigation. Inspect current water level, hazards, wind and boat conditions before traveling.

Important: These coordinates describe documented habitat locations, not a current inspection of structure or proof that fish are holding there. SCDNR material categories do not necessarily describe every material deployed at each site. Map markers are not a replacement for navigational charts.
